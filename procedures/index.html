§ 9.4

Common Student Emails — Routing & House Answers

From the analysis of 4,992 msp-exams emails. The top student intents, and the answers the office actually gives.

  1. 01
    Routing
    • Transcript / certificate / statement requests = the single most common contact (83 of 420 student mails) — that is an FRONT DESK task, not exams; students conflate the offices. Hand to Heather's team
    • 'My exam/resit is not in my timetable' = the #2 issue. House answer: email msp-exams@ as early as possible; route to Exams, not the front desk
    • Timetable clash with another programme: ' cannot move another department's exam' — the student picks, or goes to the
    • Financial hold at an exam: contact Finance to verify the hold before deciding whether the student may sit; holds are placed before the assessment period
    • Grade disputes / fraud appeals: the student-facing route is msc-examcommittee@maastrichtuniversity.nl ( tickets look like [MSC.xxxxxx])
  2. 02
    House answers worth knowing
    • Loan laptops: MSP/ESD holds a few internet-less loan laptops (normally for exams); coordinators borrow via Gian or Heather for in-tutorial written assessments
    • A room is only booked for students formally on the list — an informal 'I need a quiet room' goes to Disability Support/BoE first, not to room booking
    • entitlements (typically +30 min) carry over to the resit automatically
    • Other colleges' approvals (e.g. UCM) are NOT automatically valid at — the must approve
    • Suspected fraud: withhold the grade (blank/TBD/NG) until the decides — a transcript entry cannot be undone; provisional marks may still show in
    • Student leaves the hall with their answer papers: no grade (recorded 0), but they ARE entitled to a resit — they participated
    • Transport strike or hall disruption: the exam proceeds; a student who genuinely cannot attend confirms to by email and gets an alternative sitting in the reflection/project week
    • Graduating students are graded FIRST, so can be recomputed and the transcript re-run before the ceremony

§ 9.1

Exam-Day Floor Rules (USC halls, AY26-27)

Agreed at the TC/HS meetings of 23 June 2026 and codified in the surveillanten booklet v1.2 (2 Jul 2026). This is what actually happens in the hall.

  1. 01
    Paper collection (Niels Kuijten's procedure, now final)
    • Two regular invigilators collect exams, formula sheets and scrap paper — one tray each; check name + student number, COUNT the pages and write the count on the presentielijst, get signatures
    • Third/fourth invigilator enforces: stop writing, pens off tables, no phones while an exam is on the table, everyone seated, students leave promptly
    • Vliegende surveillant = same tasks as a fixed invigilator, attention split across assigned blocks
    • Sorting: tray 1 = original exam tray (all exams of the block + presentielijsten on top, counted against block headcount, no-shows in a separate folder inside); tray 2 = ophaalbak (cross-stacked formula sheets, clean + used scrap, clipboards, rest)
    • Blank/uncompleted exams are ALSO counted and returned, on a separate pile (not with scrap) — best practice: collect them right after handing out
    • Always use two intake lists when anything must be collected — one invigilator per block takes too long
  2. 02
    Hall rules new or clarified for 26-27
    • Afternoon block signage may be hung during the last half hour of the morning session — only by the , quietly, in agreement between (previously forbidden)
    • Late-comer slips ('briefje voor te laat komers') carry per-faculty contact details — make sure 's line is current (it feeds what zaalmanagers hand out)
    • Every TC hangs a recognisable board in the faculty colour + name at the table, so students can be redirected from the middle of the hall
    • Smartwatch/possessions question is part of the block procedure AND asked again at the toilets; at the toilets the toezichthouder drafts the , zaalmanager checks, then to the ; in the hall the drafts it
    • Irregularity form language: Dutch student → Dutch form; every other language → English
    • The hall clock is authoritative, NOT 's own clock — only the or TC may let a student work on
    • Password resets are an invigilator-level job at the block, not an queue; warn central toetscoördinatie in advance when a large first-year cohort sits
    • Calculator lending: log issue + return on a list and chase non-returns ( practice is undocumented and flagged 'te weinig rekenmachines' — bring enough)
    • Calamiteiten: follow on-site BHV instructions; the 2019 digital calamiteitenplan is obsolete, DexUM's current flow charts replace it

§ 9.3

Out-of-Cycle Assessments (BoE-approved one-offs)

The numbered SOPs cover finals, resits, midterms and — none of this. Three cases landed on one day in July 2026 (a 1-ECTS conference paper, a third attempt, an exchange-student office sitting). Decision points below; every one was previously improvised.

  1. 01
    Decision points
    • Appraisal date: the convention is 'last day of the period the attempt fell in' — but out-of-period sittings fall in NO period, and per Art 6.1.6 the date decides the graduation month (and visa timelines). Ask the which date to use; never let default it
    • Attempt 3+: the record shows Rep. blank, Rep. 1, then a third attempt — every row arrives Blocked ✓ and must be un-blocked first; whether creates a Rep. 2 row is untested. Removing/adding resit lines = Richard Vos (IT)
    • ⚠ The 0.0 'resit-as-first-attempt' placeholder is FORBIDDEN since 6 Feb 2026: the (Katja) instructed examiners to record instead, after a 0.0 produced a phantom weighted final that was nearly read as a pass
    • Exchange students: degree MSP/EP, not 7503 — only exams knows this; say it out loud on the thread
    • What the coordinator must send: the RECOMPUTED final course grade, not the raw mark for the one-off component (we may not calculate or round). The most common way these arrive unusable — and by then the coordinator has often left
    • A standalone small-ECTS entry (e.g. 1 ECTS conference credit) follows the internship pattern: approves, ECTS require an attached assessment so a grade can be given. The mechanics are not written down — whoever does it, write down how
    • Oral/Zoom one-offs: a Q&A after a presentation 'somewhat resembles an oral exam' (Katja) — 5.6 wants a second staff member present. Never formally settled; push for one anyway
